# flash

A flash card PWA for studying on a Pixel 11 in fullscreen landscape: a hand-written index card alone on a dark desk; tap to flip, swipe for the next card; a tap on the desk brings up the two controls, one of which opens the topics. Reading only; the deck is `cards/cards.toml`, edited outside the app. Live at https://flash.patforna.com.

Patric's brief is `docs/original-request.md`: it wins over everything. The spec is `docs/plan.md` (Part 2; frozen, Part 1 records why). `README.md` is for Patric; `docs/cards.md` is the card format and rules; `docs/seed.md` is how the seed deck was made and corrected.

## Purpose and constraints

- What it's for: Patric studying system design on his phone. The deck began as his own notes (`docs/seed.md`); a card should be what he would be asked, answered as he would answer aloud.
- The first requirement: extremely smooth, snappy and polished; high quality, funky and hip.
- Reading only: no editing in the app, no write API, no auth, no accounts.
- Landscape first, on a Pixel 11 (923 × 412 CSS px in fullscreen); portrait works in a tab but isn't polished.
- KISS and YAGNI: easier to add than to remove. Plan §18 lists what is out of scope.
- British English everywhere: copy, comments, identifiers (`colourFor`).
- No dead code: remove what a change makes unused in the same change.

## Decisions

Each with its reason, from the plan; everything else is specified in its Part 2.

Reading:

- The desk shows the cards alone. The controls (the fullscreen chip and the topics chip: 44 px chalk stickers in a column along the card's left edge, `src/desk`) show for 3 s at launch and after a tap on the desk beside the card, stay while the topic drawer is open and on the end card (the moment to change topics), and go as a card moves; faded out they take no tap, so a blind tap in the corner does nothing, and a control taking the keyboard's focus shows them. Patric reads fullscreen and wants nothing there but the cards (31 Oct 2026; plan §3 had a permanent panel on the left). The video-player pattern, chosen in the 31 Oct explorations over an index tab at the screen's edge (the back gesture's zone, and never quite invisible) and a full-screen picker (a mode, and more to build).
- Topics in a drawer that slides over the desk from the left (`TopicDrawer`, a modal dialog with a scrim, opened by the topics chip or `t`, closed by Escape, a tap on the scrim or the chip again), holding a quiet list (`TopicList`): one 44 px row per topic, All first, a dot in the topic's colour, the name in Geist, its count, a tick while selected. Chosen over label-maker tape and a poster of big words: the calmest of the three, which is what Patric asked for; the real deck's 14 rows scroll by one. The drawer takes the wordmark at its foot. The topics chip names the selection ("Topics: Redis, Postgres", "all topics") and shows up to three dots in the selected topics' colours, a list glyph with none.
- Nothing on a card but its question on the front and its answer on the back, both faces ruled the same (the red head rule, blue rules at the base pitch on the front, at the step's pitch on the back): Patric asked for the topics off the cards and the front to look like the back, and the counter, the colour band and the back's repeat of the question went with them (31 Oct 2026; plan §7 had all four). The live region still says where you are.
- Multi-select with OR, nothing selected meaning all: studying two topics together is normal, and "nothing = all" removes the empty state. AND was rejected.
- One live region in the app reads out where you are ("Card 7 of 16", or the end card's text), and before there is a deck what the loading or error card says: the cards remount on every move, so a live region inside one never announces. `App` renders it once, empty, beside whichever state shows, and each state writes into it through a portal: a region that mounts with its text isn't read out, and the stage is replaced when the deck arrives.
- Topics in the file's order, coloured by declared index mod 8 (eight hues 45° apart at OKLCH L 0.80, C 0.155, the first round's riso stickers tidied so no dot outshines another: Patric's pick of 31 Oct 2026 from the palette explorations): the author groups related topics, the order never shifts, and neighbours never share a colour. `cards:check` fails a card carrying two topics of one colour (8 apart): two cards showed two mint dots, Case studies and Redis, so Redis moved beside Postgres and SQL (plan §9's line has it after Streams). The sticker list is `shared/stickers.ts`, read by the list, the chip and the check.
- The buttons for keyboards and screen readers never leave as the cards change, so focus never falls back to the page: Previous is `aria-disabled` on a pass's first card (a commit with nowhere to go does nothing), and on the end card the third button reads "Shuffle again" (plan §4 has no such label).
- Tap flips; swipe left is next, right is previous within the pass; keys add vim's `h j k l` as in Patric's plant-selection, `s` to reshuffle, `f` for fullscreen and `t` for the topics (in the drawer only `t` and Escape mean anything: the cards are under a scrim).
- A tap is under 10 px and at most 350 ms: a thumb resting on the card must not flip it when it lifts.
- The 8 ms haptic tick comes as a move is decided (a swipe let go past its threshold, a key, a button, a tap on the end card), not as its card lands about 330 ms later, when it confirms nothing and, in rapid flicks, falls on the next touch. `useSwipe`'s `onDecide` and `CardStage`'s `onMove` carry it, beyond plan §11's interfaces. A reshuffle (`s`) and a row of the topic list tick at once; a flip never.
- The flip turns around the X axis: sideways motion means navigation, so the flip must not look like one, and on a 5:3 card it sweeps the short side, which is snappier.
- The card follows the finger 1:1 from its first pixel, never clamped: a card that stops following fails "extremely smooth". While a touch may still be a tap (inside the 10 px slop), it follows a finger moving more sideways than not, but a tap's jitter never brings out a sliver of the previous card, nor stops a card still coming back (arrived meanwhile, that card is taken from its place, the drift never made up in a frame), and a tap puts it back as its flip starts: it had stood still for the slop's 10 px (1.6 mm on the Pixel) and then jumped them in one frame.
- A pass is a Fisher–Yates shuffle with no repeats, closed by an end card; a reshuffle never starts with the card just seen, and happens only at launch, at the end card, on `s` and on a selection change. The brief: "cards are just shuffled and shown randomly".
- The card on screen survives a topic change when it still matches: changing topics shouldn't throw away the card being read.
- The launch is one moment: the stack is on the desk from the first render, a blank card on top that reads "shuffling…" once the deck is 200 ms late, and the first card is always dealt onto it (220 ms), the controls showing for their first 3 s. The M3 review had filmed four cuts in half a second with the old panel, the chips popping in at the edge of vision. `src/main.tsx` starts loading both fonts at boot, since a card is measured only once both are in.
- No scrolling inside a card: validation limits, three fit steps and an end-to-end fit test over the real deck. A card that scrolls is not an index card and fights the swipe.
- Each face is measured once per page load: `useFit` keeps the step it found for each card's face. A card mounts again whenever it moves between the stage's layers (they are keyed by role), and card units make its step the same at any size; measuring forces layout after layout, inside a commit that may land under a thumb.
- Code lines at most 46 characters, never scrolled: a scroller inside a swipeable card fights the swipe.
- No first-run hint: the only user designed the gestures.

Look and motion:

- "Midnight desk": paper card, marker question, pen answer on ruled lines, highlighter strokes for bold and code, the topics' riso-bright dots. Quality from restraint, funk from colour and the hand.
- Permanent Marker for the question and Caveat for the answer and everything else on the paper (a quick pen hand, variable: 500 for the answer, 700 for bold), Geist for what is typed rather than written (the controls, the topic list): Patric found Shantell Sans "Comic Sans" (31 Oct 2026) and picked this pairing from the explorations (Klee One, my recommendation, was briefly in: he had named the wrong pair), and the plan's Monaspace Radon went with Shantell: code is written in the hand too and marked with the blue highlighter, as he asked ("the highlight is enough"). Caveat's small x-height (0.355 em) puts the answer at 26u on the 30u rules and the marker at 30u; its ascenders nearly touch the rule above. Neither hand has the arrows, so twelve glyphs of Klee One's pen (→ ← and the rest of U+2190–2199, ≠, ∞, ∕) come as a third face at the end of `--font-hand`, 1.8 kB; Caveat has ≤ ≥ ≈. Each file is a Latin subset of the upstream TTF (pyftsubset, Google's latin range plus the arrows): 78, 29, 2 and 35 kB, 142 of the 240 kB font budget.
- The card being read stays upright; only the blank cards peeking behind it tilt: crisp text.
- The flip's perspective is the frame's, and only while a flip runs, departing from plan §4 (`perspective(1400px)` in the flipper's own transform): at rest that projection had Chromium resample the card being read on the Pixel, its glyph edges smeared over two device pixels and its rules fuzzy. Frame and flipper share their centre, so the turn is the same (a frame 60 ms in differs in 0.07 % of its pixels).
- The flip takes 260 ms on `cubic-bezier(0.4, 1.1, 0.65, 1)`, departing from plan §4's 380 ms on `cubic-bezier(0.3, 1.2, 0.45, 1)`: that curve is within 1° of flat by 190 ms but overshoots 2.25° and lands only at 380 ms, and Chromium resamples the card for as long as the transition runs, so the answer lay still yet soft for about 190 ms (2.5 times the anti-aliased edge pixels of the card at rest), then snapped sharp. The new curve stays within 1.2° of the old for the first 200 ms, overshoots 0.43° and lands at 260 ms; the perspective and the blank card underneath (`data-flipping`) end 120 ms sooner.
- The end card lies on both blank cards, the pass it closes, ready to shuffle (plan §7 thins the stack to none): alone on the near-black desk it read as a pale rectangle, and no shadow shows there. They come with the move to it and go with the move back from it (the stage's `data-closing` and `data-opening`, set as the move is decided), not once it lands: two cards appearing under a card already settled read as a late pop.
- List markers in the answer's margin are in `--pen-pink` (#d02a68, 4.9:1 on paper), not plan §7's sticker pink (2.4:1): a numbered list's numerals are text. Text contrast is tested over the tokens (`src/styles/contrast.test.ts`), since axe can't see it on this page.
- Bold and inline code are highlighter strokes, yellow and blue (`--hl`, `--mark-code`), drawn as layered gradients after a marker Patric had drawn for another page (31 Oct 2026): translucent ink heavier where the marker was put down and lifted, a darker top edge, unevenly rounded ends, and a second, shorter swipe a little higher and to the left, so the top edge is ragged; 0.95 em tall over the x-height and the baseline, so ascenders show above it; multiplied with the paper, so the rules show through. A code block gets the blue rubbed over its whole slip. Broken over two lines they are sliced, not cloned as plan §7 has it (`box-decoration-break`): in Chromium a cloned inline turns `text-wrap: pretty` off for its whole block, which then ends on a lone word, as 7 backs of the real deck did. The fit allows a highlight's 2u of end padding past the column, which had pushed two backs to step 1 over one invisible pixel.
- The card is designed in card units (600 × 360, `--u`, the `frame` has `container-type: size`): it lays out identically at any size.
- The press scales `lift`, not the dragged layer: the card never trails the finger.
- A control's or a row's press, like the card's, comes from pointer events (`data-pressed` from pointerdown until pointerup, cancel or leave: `src/panel/press.ts`, shared by the controls and the rows), not from `:active`: a touch screen sets that only once a quick tap has lifted and holds it 150 ms, so the push played after the thumb had gone. `:active` pushes a control only with the keyboard's focus (Space); kept for touches too, it held it in for 180 ms after the lift.
- Flings, departing from plan §4 after the M3 review: a released card flies half its width past −(cardRect.right + 80), on a curve worked out for that distance, so it slows down off screen instead of crawling over the panel; keys and buttons still fly to −(right + 80). A card still flying out when its move lands early (a touch, a key, a button), or when a deal replaces it (`s`, a topic change), flies on as a copy (no `data-role`, inert, `aria-hidden`) taken as it was flung, so in rapid flicks no card vanishes mid-air: a deal's render has already put the new card in its layer. The end card, with nothing under it, lands its move as it is let go: the next pass is dealt under it as it flies off. Every way off it does the same (a tap, which its hint offers, the keys that flip, its "Shuffle again" button, `s`), on the key-commit curve: it had vanished in one frame.
- Input landing a move early never snaps the card leaving or the card coming back, departing from plan §4, where a pointerdown finishes any commit, cancel or deal at once. Other motions it still ends at once, since a touch on the stage, a key or a button first lands whatever runs (`finishRunning`, from `down()`, `commit()`, `tap()` and `reshuffle()`): the card rising under a card flung away completes its rise as it takes the top (up to 8 px and 3.5 % of its size); a spring back that no touch on the card catches puts the cards home; a deal puts its card in place (the rest of its 14 px rise and its fade) and the card under it in its own. A flip still turning ends at once as a drag is recognised (plan §4) or a key or a button moves the card, which would otherwise travel edge-on, and a deal (`s`, or a topic change the card on screen doesn't survive) drops what was in flight with its pass, but for a card flying out. The card leaving flies on as a copy (above). A card coming back when its move lands early (a touch, a key, a button) carries on into its place from where it was, and the card it covers settles into its own in the time left; a third move back, landing the second early, settles that card from where it is into its place under the next, over a copy of the card it was covering, which leaves the layers then (it had jumped up to 160 px in one frame as that card vanished); the next touch leaves them be unless it moves that card (a left drag, once past the slop, or a key going on), which takes hold of it where it is; it then leans only as it is dragged on from there, and dragged back it takes the card it covers back into its own place, so nothing moves in a frame as the card before takes over. A touch on a card springing back catches it where it is, and the drag goes on from there (a tap springs it back from there). The card coming back had jumped up to 340 px in one frame; the card springing back jumped home under the thumb, which then held it as far from where it had caught it.
- The previous card waits with its right edge on the screen's left edge, at −cardRect.right, not plan §4's −(right + 80): a right drag shows it from the first pixel, where the plan's moved nothing for the first 80 px (about 13 mm).
- A deal also settles the card underneath, from the top card's place to its own (`scale(1)` to `scale(0.965) translateY(8px)`) under the card dealt, beyond plan §4's deal: it had shrunk back in one frame as each deal began, the loading card's place most visibly.
- While a flip or a deal runs, the card underneath shows blank paper (the stage's `data-flipping`, `data-dealing`): near 90° the flipping card is almost edge-on, and a dealt card fades in over it, so the next question must not show through. The deal's is a departure from plan §11, which blanks it for flips only: the M3 review saw both questions superimposed.
- The end card's tick draws itself as the move to it is decided (CSS on its two strokes under the stage's `data-closing`: the short one, 110 ms, then the long one, 200 ms; transforms only), so the pass is ticked off as the last card leaves, and it is drawn before the move lands (320 ms); an addition to plan §4's motion list: the end card is the app's one reward, closing every pass, and was static. Drawn only once the move landed, it started about 255 ms after the end card showed, a late second event. Under the last card otherwise it waits hidden; the copy flying off keeps it drawn; reduced motion shows it drawn; a move landed early completes it.
- Reduced motion: animations become short fades, but the drag still follows the finger, without rotation; direct manipulation is not motion.
- The fullscreen chip is the way in from a browser tab and, once the tab is fullscreen, the way out (the brackets pointing in): the installed app is already fullscreen (`display: fullscreen`), locked to landscape, with nothing to leave, so it shows neither. On the phone there had been no visible way out (31 Oct 2026; plan §3 has none). Icon-only, as every control: the names are labels.
- The controls stand in a column along the card's left edge, the fullscreen chip level with its top edge and the topics chip with its foot, above the stage, which takes every touch on the desk (a card flung off passes under them). The shell's one row is exactly as tall as the card, `min(100dvh − 48px, (100vw − left − right − 2 × 68px) × 3/5)`, centred, with the controls' column and its mirror padded off on both sides, so the card stays centred and a wide screen, whose card fills the stage's width, never puts it under a control. A camera cutout narrows the card; the controls follow.
- The list's foot fades only while there is more below, beyond plan §3, whose 24 px fade never lifts: none while every row fits, 24 px while the list can scroll further, going out over the last 24 px of the scroll (over all of it when there is less), so the last row shows whole at the end. Drawn always (a mask on the scroller's own box), the fade had left the last row half faded even at the end, so the fit test refused any deck whose chips did not all fit clear of it; it checks the scroll instead. No JavaScript: the mask's length is a registered `--fade` (initial 0) that the list's own scroll animates (`animation-timeline: scroll(self)`, `animation-range: max(0px, 100% - 24px) 100%`); with nothing to scroll the timeline is inactive and the fade stays at 0. Animated over the whole scroll instead, it would thin while plenty was still below (12 px half way down 20 topics' 98 px scroll).

Stack:

- Gestures and animation are hand-written (pointer events, transforms written straight to the element, the Web Animations API for flings, a CSS transition for the flip), with no motion library: `motion/react` with drag costs about 45 kB gzipped on top of React's 68 kB.
- Plain CSS with tokens and CSS Modules; no Tailwind, no router; TanStack Query for the one fetch.
- Hono on `Bun.serve`, and `hc<Api>` in the client over the routes' inferred types: end-to-end types with no code generation, plus static serving and `app.request()` for tests without a port.
- No OpenAPI chain: openapi-typescript crashes under TypeScript 7 and openapi-fetch is in maintenance mode (Patric: "don't get hung up on openapi-fetch").
- TOML for the deck, parsed by smol-toml: a standard grammar whose errors carry line and column; Bun's own parser doesn't comply (Gotchas).
- Markdown rendered on the server by `Bun.markdown.html` behind an allowlist of tags: no Markdown dependency and no parser in the bundle.
- The renderer puts U+2060 (word joiner, invisible) after a hyphen inside a word and after every hyphen in inline code: lines broke inside "staff-level" and `allkeys-lfu`, CSS can't forbid a break after a hyphen, and neither font has a non-breaking hyphen. The glyph check skips it. It also holds inline code together with U+00A0 (no-break space, which both fonts draw) where a break would split it: a span of up to 20 characters, a word of one or two at either end of a longer one, a quoted value of up to 18 (lines had ended on a lone `ON`, or split `hash(value) = 42` and `'ANY 1 (s1, s2)'`). Outside code, a range keeps its en dash (2–4: a word joiner after it) and a number the word it counts (7 days, 1 s, 100k writes: a no-break space), unless the number is part of a word or a version (s3, v1.2): lines had ended on "the default is 7" and on a highlight's "2–". An arrow keeps what it points at (a no-break space after it): a line may break before it, never after it, where two had ended on an arrow pointing at nothing. Outside code, an apostrophe or closing quote after a letter or digit becomes ’ and any other single quote ‘ (the end and error cards' copy is curly too): Shantell, the hand at the time, drew the typewriter ' as an upright tick on the back and a slanted one on the front, so a word changed shape as the card turned over, beside the deck's curly double quotes.
- A service worker (vite-plugin-pwa, `generateSW`): instant start of the installed app and offline reading; the deck is fetched network-first, so cards stay fresh. An HTTP error counts as a network failure (a `fetchDidSucceed` plugin throws on it), so the cached deck also stands in for Caddy's 502 while the server is down; Workbox alone passes the error to the app.
- One package, no workspaces; exact pins at least 7 days old (`bunfig.toml`).

Data:

- A card is `question`, `topics` and `answer`, as Patric specified. Its id derives from the question: ids would only matter for spaced repetition, which is out of scope. No version key in the file: nothing would read it.
- Validation is strict and reports every problem at once; the server refuses to start on a bad deck.
- A question is 1–100 characters with a letter or digit for its id, not plan §9's 8–100: the brief's front is "a short sentence (question, tell me about..., topic, etc.)", so a topic alone ("TLS") is one.
- An answer has at most 11 lines, not plan §9's 12: the smallest fit step holds 11.5 rules, so a 12-line answer passed `cards:check` and could only scroll, which the fit test fails at ship time.
- The source checks (an unclosed fence, `[label]:` lines, the 46 characters of code) read the lines as Bun's md4c does with indented code off, through one reader (`readLines` in `server/markdown.ts`): at any indentation, a fence closed only by a line of the same character, at least as long, with nothing after it. Not plan §9's count of fence lines: four backticks closed by three, a fence closed by ```` ``` x ````, or an indented fence passed the check and turned the rest of the answer into code.
- Corrections of the source notes live in TOML comments and `docs/seed.md`, never on a card: commentary about the source is not study material. The notes' stubs get no cards.
- The deck's version (the first 12 characters of the base64url SHA-256 of its canonical JSON) is the ETag and what `/healthz` reports, so a deploy can confirm what it shipped.

Hosting:

- Its own droplet, `flash` in fra1, Caddy in front with `encode zstd gzip`, the server bundled into one `server.js` with no `node_modules`: in line with Patric's other apps.
- `deploy-do.sh up` runs `bun install --frozen-lockfile && bun run verify` first, and no flag skips it: there is no CI, so the gate lives in the deploy.
- `deploy-do.sh cards` ships the deck without code: the most frequent change gets a fast path (about 30 s) that can't touch the code. It ships only onto the code it checked the deck against, and puts the last deck back if the server refuses the new one: the M3 review alone changed the rules or the renderer five times, each enough for a deck checked on other code to crash the server or fail the version check.
- Bun on the droplet is pinned to the laptop's `bun --version`: the droplet runs what the gate tested, and `Bun.markdown` is unstable.
- No robots.txt: a crawler that is disallowed never sees the `noindex`, so `X-Robots-Tag` and the meta tag do the work.

## Conventions

- Strict Beck-style TDD: a failing test first, the simplest change to green, refactor at green, commit at green. Type declarations, configuration, CSS and pure wiring need no test.
- Braced effect bodies only: `useEffect(() => { … })`, never `useEffect(() => expr)`. A returned Promise is called as cleanup and blanks the app; `tests/conventions.test.ts` fails on it.
- Measure text only after `document.fonts.load()`, never on `document.fonts.status`, which reads "loaded" before any face is requested.
- Every visible control is at least 44 × 44 px under a coarse pointer.
- Nothing scrolls sideways at any size: `document.documentElement.scrollWidth === innerWidth`.
- Animate only `transform` and `opacity` (never `box-shadow`). The one exception, the topic list's fade (`--fade` in its mask), moves only with the list's own scroll, never on the clock.
- No React state during a drag: `pointermove` writes transforms to the element, and a test asserts zero React commits.
- Nothing draggable in the 48 px strip on the right; drags start only on the card; the topic drawer takes taps and its own scroll only.
- Set type through `var(--font-hand)`, `var(--font-marker)` and `var(--font-ui)`; never name a family.
- Every size on the card is a multiple of `--u`, used only by the frame's descendants.
- Tests sit beside their module as `x.test.ts(x)`. Import Vitest's APIs explicitly: its globals are on only so Testing Library cleans up after each test.
- Biome with `--error-on-warnings`: two spaces, double quotes, lines up to 120.
- Playwright runs headless only; never a headed browser or anything that opens a window or plays sound.

- The stage and the card meet at a DOM contract (plan §11), outer to inner: `stage` › `layer[data-role="prev|current|next"]` › `lift` › `frame` › `flipper` › two `face`s. `CardStage` takes a `renderCard` prop, so it never imports the card.
- `server/api.ts` imports nothing Bun-specific: the client imports its type.
- `tsconfig.json` is the browser program (DOM and `vite/client`, no Bun or Node types): `src`, `shared`, `tests/conventions.test.ts`, and `server/api.ts` through the client's type import. `tsconfig.node.json` is the Bun program (`bun` types, plus the DOM lib for `page.evaluate` callbacks): `server`, `scripts`, `shared`, `tests/e2e`, the config files. Both: TypeScript 7 (native `tsc`), `strict`, `noUncheckedIndexedAccess`, `verbatimModuleSyntax` (types via `import type`), bundler resolution, extensionless imports.
- Hono: `import { serveStatic } from "@hono/bun"` on the server; `import { hc } from "hono/client"` with `hc<Api>` in the client.
- Fonts: `"Caveat"` (variable, `font-weight` 400–700), `"Permanent Marker"` (400), `"Klee One Arrows"` (400, twelve glyphs) and `"Geist"` (variable, 100–900), declared in `src/styles/fonts.css`, each a Latin subset of the upstream TTF made with `uvx --from fonttools --with brotli pyftsubset <ttf> --unicodes=<Google's latin range>,U+2190-2199,U+21A9-21AA,U+2212,U+2215,U+221E,U+2248,U+2260,U+2264-2265 --layout-features='*' --flavor=woff2` (the arrows file: `--unicodes=U+2190-2199,U+21A9-21AA,U+2260,U+221E,U+2215`). `index.html` preloads the two hands from `/src/fonts/`, which Vite rewrites to the hashed `/assets/` URLs the CSS uses.
- Tokens are custom properties on `:root` in `src/styles/tokens.css`, including `--flip-duration`, `--flip-easing` and `--flip-fade-duration`.

## Shipping cards versus code

- **Cards**: edit `cards/cards.toml`, `bun run cards:check`, commit and push, then `scripts/deploy-do.sh cards`: the check, a build (about a second), a stop unless the droplet runs exactly that code (Bun's version and the checksum of every file of `server.js` and `dist/`, the same command on both sides: on other code a deck the droplet refuses, or renders to another version, would pass), the fit test on the real deck (`tests/e2e/fit.spec.ts`, which the script runs by name: every card fits both faces, every row of the topic list is in full view in the drawer or, when they outgrow it, scrolls into it with no fade left at the end, at 412, 356 and 332 px), one file uploaded with the last deck kept as `cards.toml.prev`, a restart, and the health check; should the server refuse the deck all the same, the last one goes back and the server restarts, so the app is never left down. It never ships code. The phone shows the new deck on the next launch.
- **Code**: commit and push, then `scripts/deploy-do.sh up`: the gate, `build/release/` staged (`server.js`, `dist/`, `cards/cards.toml`) and sent with one `rsync -az --delete`, a restart, and the health check. The phone runs the new code one launch late.
- **The health check**: `/healthz` on the droplet first (its journal printed if the server won't start), then https://flash.patforna.com/healthz from the laptop, whose `version` must equal `bun scripts/check-cards.ts --version cards/cards.toml`, retried for up to 2 minutes while a new droplet's DNS record and certificate come up.
- `link` prints the address, `logs` the server's journal; `down` deletes the droplet and its A record.

## Gotchas

- **Bun's TOML parser**: `Bun.TOML.parse` (1.3.14) keeps the line break after an opening `'''`, which TOML 1.0 trims, and its errors carry no line or column. Hence smol-toml.
- **openapi-typescript and TypeScript 7**: openapi-typescript 7.13.0 crashes under TypeScript 7.0.2 (`ts.factory` is gone; upstream #2841). TypeScript 7's main export is only its version (the compiler API sits under `typescript/unstable/*`), so the conventions test reads the sources with regexes.
- **`Bun.markdown` is unstable**: Bun is pinned and the renderer tested. Tables, strikethrough and task lists are on by default despite Bun's docs, so they are switched off; `hardSoftBreaks` does nothing in 1.3.14, so the renderer turns line breaks into `<br>` itself.
- **The Android back gesture**: in the installed fullscreen app, a swipe from either edge means back (up to 40 dp per side, with no web opt-out). Hence the 48 px strip on the right, drags only from the card, a drawer that takes taps and its own scroll only, and no index tab at the edge. Emulation can't show it: check on the phone.
- **The fullscreen chip in Playwright**: every Playwright page is `display-mode: browser` with fullscreen enabled (CDP can't emulate the display mode), so the chip always shows, as the way in. A key-triggered `requestFullscreen` does switch headless Chromium to `display-mode: fullscreen`, so `pixel11` screenshots and layout checks press `f` first.
- **The hostname is public**: it is in the Certificate Transparency logs from the moment Caddy gets its certificate. `noindex` only keeps it out of search results; never put private material in cards.
- **Caddy's one-line blocks**: Caddy can't parse a site block written on one line (`{` must end its line and `}` stand on its own), so the deploy script writes it over several lines.
- **A new name's missing answer is cached**: on the first `up`, a lookup of `flash.patforna.com` made before DigitalOcean's nameservers had the new record left the laptop's resolver caching "no such name" for the zone's negative TTL (30 minutes, the SOA's last field), so the 2-minute health check failed although the app was serving. The check now pins the name to the droplet's address (`curl --resolve`), which still proves Caddy, the certificate and the deck; public DNS is checked with `dig @ns1.digitalocean.com`.
- **The service-worker kill switch**: if a broken worker strands the phone, add `selfDestroying: true` to the `VitePWA` options in `vite.config.ts` and redeploy; the new worker removes itself and its caches.
- **A code change shows one launch late**: `registerSW.js` only registers, so the new worker installs in the background while the open page keeps the old bundle. A card change shows on the next launch, since the deck is fetched network-first. The deck is cached only once a controlled page has fetched it: the first load's preload runs before the worker controls the page.
- `document.getAnimations()` always lists the topic list's fade, a scroll-driven CSS animation, `running` even with nothing to scroll: what moves is what runs on `document.timeline`, which is all `settled()` and `animated()` count. Playwright's `animations: "disabled"` cancels it for a screenshot (its end time is a percentage, not a number), so no screenshot shows the fade.
- `animation.finish()` notifies late (`finished` a microtask later, `onfinish` a frame later): call `finish()`, then complete the commit synchronously with `flushSync` before reading the layers.
- A Web Animation left pending starts on the next frame, from its first keyframe: a released card stood still for a frame before it flew. Moves set `startTime` to `document.timeline.currentTime` (the last frame's time, 2–16 ms old); the deal doesn't, since after the Reader's first render the timeline is 30–60 ms old and the deal would start a quarter of the way in. A CSS transition starts pending too, so the flip's first frame stood unturned: the stage starts the transitions a change of side sets off at the timeline's time as well (asking for them with `getAnimations()` creates them; the timeline is 0–15 ms old at a flip).
- A finished animation that fills stays in `document.getAnimations()` and overrides inline transforms, freezing the next drag: every animation ends at its resting style, or writes its end state inline and is cancelled in the frame it ends.
- The flip's `perspective: 1400px` is on the `frame`, the flipper's direct parent (on the stage it would reach only the stage's children), and only while a flip runs (`[data-flipping] [data-role="current"] .frame`): at rest any perspective, even over `rotateX(0)`, has Chromium draw the card through a projection, which at the Pixel's DPR 2.625 (not at 2 or 3) resamples the writing: a crop of a front had 2.1 times the anti-aliased edge pixels, of a back 1.3 to 1.7. `overflow`, `opacity`, `filter`, `clip-path`, `mask` or `contain: paint` on the flipper flatten it and break `backface-visibility`.
- `--u` on the `frame` element itself resolves against the stage: use it only in the frame's descendants, and never register it with `@property`.
- Inline code needs `line-height: 0`, or each line with code grows by about 2 px and the writing drifts off the rules.
- Neither hand has the arrows (→ ←): `--font-hand` ends in "Klee One Arrows", twelve glyphs of Klee One's pen, and `--font-marker` falls back through Caveat to it. Permanent Marker lacks ≤ ≥ too and falls back to Caveat for them. `code` and `pre` inherit the hand explicitly: the browser's own stylesheet would set them in monospace.
- Measure interaction latency with Event Timing (`PerformanceObserver({ type: "event" })`), not a DevTools trace, which inflates DOM-churn timings.
- The Event Timing budget counts interactions, as INP does: the entries with an `interactionId`, the slowest of each. A touch's `pointerover` and `pointerout` are no interaction, only pipeline, and alone took the worst entry to 48 ms and over. Measured over 40 runs after the M3 review (and the same on the code before it): median 40 ms, 48 ms in a quarter of runs, 56 ms once; the margin is one 8 ms tick of Event Timing's rounding.
- Event Timing in headless Chromium is mostly pipeline: a tap's touch events wait about a frame before they are dispatched and are presented one or two frames later, so at 4× CPU a tap's entries read 16–40 ms, of which the app's own processing is about 8 ms. While a measured frame is on its way, leave the page alone: `settled()` polls `document.getAnimations()`, which forces style into the frames being measured.
- CDP answers `Input.dispatchTouchEvent` for a move before the page sees it (moves are dispatched with the next frame): poll for the effect of the last move rather than reading it at once.
- A test that lands input on a card in flight slows the page's animations to a quarter first (`slowMotion` in `tests/e2e/helpers.ts`, DevTools' playback rate): at full speed a touch arriving 100 ms late on a busy machine missed the moment, and such tests failed or passed without testing anything. The slowed clock is `document.timeline`'s and also stamps `requestAnimationFrame`, so speeds measured from frame times are in the animations' time; timers, `performance.now()` and input keep real time.
- happy-dom 21 has no `document.fonts`, no `document.getAnimations` and no layout; `(pointer: coarse)` is false; `fullscreenEnabled`, `navigator.vibrate` and `navigator.wakeLock` are undefined; `PointerEvent` ignores `timeStamp` in its init (set it with `Object.defineProperty`). Stub or inject these; everything visual belongs to Playwright.
- Outside CI, Playwright reuses a server already listening on 4173 or 4174: a stale one makes the tests use an old build or deck. `deploy-do.sh` runs both its Playwright gates with `CI=1`, so a busy port fails the deploy instead (it also makes Playwright's reporter print dots).
- `hono/bun`'s `serveStatic` is deprecated (gone in v5): use `@hono/bun`'s. Neither sends an ETag for static files.
- Vitest's persistent module cache (`fsModuleCache`) is off: it kept a test's `import.meta.glob` expansion until the test file itself changed, so the conventions test never saw a new source file.
